INTRODUCTION 
Environmental Inspection & Control Services (EICS) conducted a pre-demolition asbestos survey at 1220 
Idaho Circle, Tonopah, NV on September 19, 2023.  Sample collection of suspect asbestos containing 
materials included only readily visible and accessible materials from the interior areas of the site that are 
planned for renovation. 
The conclusions of this analysis are based on a study limited to the specific areas investigated and 
laboratory analyses of samples that were collected from those specific areas.  EICS renders no opinion as 
to the condition of areas of the subject property that were not investigated.  
OBJECTIVE 
The purpose of this asbestos survey was to sample and analyze suspect asbestos-containing materials 
(ACM) which could pose a health-hazard, when disturbed by site work activities associated with the 
planned demolition. 
SCOPE OF WORK 
Samples of suspect asbestos-containing materials (ACM) were collected from the project site to determine 
asbestos content.  Suspect ACM was sampled according to the guidelines set forth in 40 CFR Part 763. 
Upon collection, the samples were delivered to an accredited laboratory, Hayes Microbial Consulting in 
Midlothian, VA and analyzed using the Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) method in accordance with the 
EPA reference method 600/R-93/116.  
Non-friable sources of asbestos are materials containing cement or asphalt binder which may become 
friable and release fibers if the sources are exposed to actions such as abrasion, drilling, cutting, fracturing 
or hammering.  Non-friable sources of asbestos do not typically pose a significant exposure risk if they 
remain in good condition and are not disturbed.  During demolition activities, non-friable sources may 
become friable and thus may pose an exposure risk. 
The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) regulation 29 CFR 1926.1101 requires certain 
construction materials to be presumed to contain asbestos, for purposes of this regulation. All thermal 
system insulation (TSI), surfacing material, and asphalt/vinyl flooring that are present in a building 
constructed prior to 1980 and have not been appropriately tested are "presumed asbestos-containing 
material". 
FIELD ACTIVITIES 
The asbestos survey was conducted by Lauren Speelman, an accredited asbestos inspector. 
EICS began the asbestos sampling activities with a visual assessment, identification and inventory of 
readily visible and of additional suspect ACM that was discovered at the project site.  A homogeneous 
area consists of building materials that appear similar throughout in terms of color, texture and date of 
application.  Materials identified as glass, wood, metal, plastic or rubber were not considered to be 
suspect ACM.

A physical assessment of each homogeneous area of suspect ACM at the project site was conducted to 
assess the friability and condition of the materials.  A friable material is defined by the EPA as a material 
which can be crumbled, pulverized or reduced to powder by hand pressure when dry.  Friability was 
assessed by physically touching suspect materials.  
Six bulk samples of suspect ACM were collected from the project site.  Bulk samples of suspect ACM were 
collected in general accordance with AHERA sampling protocols using wet methods as applicable to 
reduce the potential for fiber release.  Samples were placed in sealable containers and labeled with unique 
sample numbers using an indelible marker.  
Although reasonable effort was made to survey all accessible suspect materials, additional suspect 
asbestos containing materials could be located within walls, in voids or in other concealed areas of the 
project site. 
Sampling locations are depicted in Appendix B.  The suspect materials are tabulated in Table 1. Copies of 
the analytical reports and chain-of-custody forms are provided in Appendix A.

FINDINGS AND CONCLUSIONS 
Laboratory results indicate following materials contain asbestos exceeding 1%.  According to OSHA (29 
CFR 1926.1101) removal of these materials is Class I or II work.  These materials may only be disturbed 
by a State licensed asbestos abatement contractor.  Only a final visual inspection will be required 
following abatement if the building will be immediately demolished.  In the event that the buildings are 
not immediately demolished, clearance air monitoring will be required.  During demolition, an “asbestos 
competent person” will be required to be onsite to identify any suspect asbestos containing materials 
that were previously undiscovered during the original inspection.
