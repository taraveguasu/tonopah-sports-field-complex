COMMUNICATIONS - GENERAL
PART  1  GENERAL
1.1 
OVERVIEW
A. 
Contractor is expected to furnish and install a complete and operational network cabling 
infrastructure (cable and connectivity) for Technology-related systems.  Connectivity 
includes jacks, modular plugs, copper and fiber patch panels, fiber optic connectors, pre-
terminated copper and fiber optic patch cords, and fiber optic insert panels (couplers for 
panels), and faceplates. Cable would include the fiber optic cable used for backbone runs to 
HS and Copper Category cable used for cameras, IP speakers, and Wireless Access Points.
1. 
This also includes furnishing and installing networking equipment (network switches) 
and associated UPS (uninterruptible power supplies) to support the equipment 
plugged into the switches (IP Clock/Intercom Speakers, Wireless Access Points, 
computer workstations or VoIP phones, and surveillance cameras). 
a. 
Due to exact loads not being known, contractor shall use the wattage of the 
power supply for a given switch when calculating run time.
2. 
The Video Surveillance System cameras are required to be installed on a parallel 
network with separate fiber optic strands in the backbone connecting to a different 
switch in the ER at the HS.  Coordinate with Owner for configuration information, IP 
address schemes and ranges, VLANS, and which switch and switch port(s) to use 
back at the HS.  Contractor shall not patch cameras into same switch being used for 
the IP speakers, wireless access points, or workstation/VoIP ports.
B. 
Contractor shall furnish and install all fiber optic patch cords and copper patch cords of the 
appropriate length, and match the performance of the patch panels and horizontal copper or 
backbone fiber optic cable and connector type being interconnected.

PART  3  EXECUTION
3.1 
INSTALLATION
A. 
Category 6A Cabling.
1. 
Cabling shall be installed above ceilings in conduit or j-hooks.  Conduit pathways shall 
be required in rooms which have exposed structure above, or when cable has to pass 
over hard lid ceilings.
2. 
Cabling in plenum spaces shall be rated appropriately.
B. 
Backbone Underground
1. 
Cabling shall be rated appropriately for OSP installation.
2. 
Use factory conduit sweeps, with a minimum radius 6x the diameter of the conduit.
3. 
Installation shall be compliant with TIA-758.
C. 
Fiber Optic Cabling
1. 
Terminate all multimode fiber optic cables as required with an LC connector. Land the 
LC connectors in the fiber interconnect center LC adapter plate.
2. 
All fiber optic LC connectors shall be capable of achieving an insertion loss of .30 dB 
or less which is guaranteed under the warranty.
3. 
Utilize outside plant rated cable whenever cable runs are routed through conduit 
under grade or outside the building envelope.
4. 
Secure all cables at termination points to provide strain relief.  Care shall be taken 
never to bend, crush, or kink cables.  Support all fiber optic cables by their outer 
jacket.  Never allow the fiber to be bent, hung, or supported in such a manner as to

3.2 
COORDINATION
A. 
Regardless of progress of overall installation, the network switches shall not be installed until 
the following is completed in the new IT room:
1. 
Power – finished, tested, no further planned outages, and all outlets operational.
2. 
Climate controlled – HVAC for room commissioned and functioning properly 24/7 with 
no further planned outages.  Temperature in room shall be maintained at a constant 
level required by Owner.
3. 
Cabling is installed and tested with passing results.
4. 
All labeling has been completed.
5. 
All cabinets, power supplies, PDUs, and UPS has all been installed.
6. 
Permanent lighting for the room is installed and functioning correctly.
7. 
Room can be secured and can be considered protected/secured.  Doors to room must 
be installed.
8. 
Room is sealed – wall penetrations, open ceiling tiles, underground conduit sleeve 
openings have been sealed to prevent ingress of vermin.
9. 
All wall and floor finishing has been completed.
10. 
Room has been thoroughly cleaned, with no equipment obstructing access to front 
and rear of racks.  All wall mounted equipment and brackets shall be wiped clean of 
any dust or debris.
B. 
Provide estimated dates equipment will be required so Owner can purchase cameras and 
wireless access points and schedule resources far enough in advance to avoid any conflicts 
or delays.  Coordination should not begin until after shop drawings have been approved.
C. 
For any existing equipment being relocated/reused, dates of outage as equipment is moved 
from old location to new location must be coordinated in conjunction with Architect and 
Owner.
1. 
Be aware of when operation of Public Address loudspeakers are expected by the 
school.
2. 
Attempt to make relocation occur over school holidays, provided it will be operational 
when staff and students return.
a. 
All testing and adjusting must also be complete.

3.4 
TESTING
A. 
GENERAL
1. 
The Contractor shall resolve all tests that result in a FAIL prior to submitting to Owner 
for acceptance.  Troubleshoot and resolve any failures.  Most failures attributed to 
construction are going to occur at excessive bending points or at the connector.
2. 
Submit test reports with summary report in pdf format, including all labels from each 
test.
3. 
Submit full test report for each in pdf format.
4. 
Native file test reports shall be retained and provided to the A/E upon request if there 
are discrepancies or anomalies discovered in the test reports.
5. 
Testing shall conform to ANSI/TIA-568.0-E standard.
6. 
The structure for commercial building cabling is based on the generic cabling system 
structure specified in TIA-568-0.
7. 
Performance and technical criteria for balanced twisted-pair cabling systems are 
specified in TIA-568.2.
8. 
All testing shall be performed using the Fluke Versiv Family of Cabling Testing 
products. (DSX 8000/5000 for Copper and CFP/OFP Quad for fiber or above).
9. 
A factory calibration report shall show calibration performed within twelve months of 
the test report date and shall be included with the cable testing report package.
10. 
Provide UTP Category 6A and Optical Fiber OTDR Test Reports.
11. 
Provide Optical Fiber Power Loss Test Reports.
12. 
Test results which report a PASS*, FAIL*, or FAIL result for any of the parameters 
shall not be accepted.  It is incumbent upon the Contractor to determine the cause of 
the issue, perform corrective action, and retest prior to submittal of test reports to the 
owner.  The Contractor shall coordinate directly with the manufacturer if they are 
unable to resolve the issue in the field.

1. 
Tests of multimode fiber optic cable shall be conducted utilizing an OTDR (Optical 
Time Domain Reflectometer with an integrated optical light source and optical power 
meter) at 850nm and 1300nm with the end-to-end attenuation in db/km recorded for 
each fiber.
2. 
Contractor shall perform Tier I and Tier II testing of fiber optic cable using the Fluke 
CertiFiber Pro OLTS (Optical Loss Test Set) for length and loss, and the OptiFiber Pro 
OTDR (Optical Time Domain Reflectometer) to show reflection of any imperfections or 
major “events” which contribute to loss. Maximum allowable fiber optic LC connector 
insertion loss (mated pair of connectors) shall be .30dB.
a. 
Test wavelengths in both directions, using one-cord reference method per 
ANSI/TIA-526-14-C-2015. This testing procedure is also referred to as the one-
jumper reference method.
b. 
Contractor shall use reference grade patch cords from manufacturer of the 
cable being used or those recommended by the manufacturer of the tester.
c. 
Contractor shall only use the encircled flux test jumpers.  Do not use mandrels 
to set reference for multimode testing.
