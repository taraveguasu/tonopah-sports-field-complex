2.2   
 
 
HINGING METHODS 
 
A. 
Drawings typically depict doors at 90 degrees, doors will actually swing to maximum 
allowable degree of opening potential and if it’s a pair, maximum of the two.  Use wide-
throw conventional or continuous hinges as needed up to 8 inches in width to allow door 
to stand parallel to wall for true 180-degree opening.  Advise architect if 8-inch width is 
insufficient. 
B. 
Conform to manufacturer’s published hinge selection standard for door dimensions, 
weight and frequency, and to hinge selection as scheduled.  Where manufacturer’s 
standard exceeds the scheduled product, furnish the heavier of the two choices, and 
notify Architect of deviation from scheduled hardware. 
 
C. 
Conventional Hinges: Steel or stainless steel pins and concealed bearings. Hinge open 
widths minimum, but of sufficient throw to permit maximum door swing.  In general,

2.4   
 
 
EXIT DEVICES / PANIC HARDWARE 
 
A. 
General features: 
1. 
End caps: impact-resistant, flush-mounted.  No raised edges or lips to catch carts 
or other equipment. 
2. 
Releasable in normal operation with 15-lb. maximum operating force and with 32 
lb. maximum pressure under 250-lb. load to the door. 
3. 
Flush end cap design as opposed to typical "bottle-cap" design end cap. 
4. 
Exit Device Series and Design:  Von Duprin 98 Series (smooth mechanism case) 
with 996 lever trim 
 
B. 
Specific Features: 
1. 
Non-Fire Rated Devices: Hex Key Dogging 
2. 
Lever Trim: 996 breakaway type, forged brass or bronze escutcheon minimum 
.130” thickness, compression spring drive, match lockset lever design. 
a. 
Use standard Von Duprin 996 trim only at fire rated openings.   All other 
openings to be provided with Vandal Resistant Pull Trim, Ives VR900 
series or reviewed equal. 
3. 
Fire-Labeled Devices: UL label indicating “Fire Exit Hardware”.  Vertical rod 
devices less bottom rod unless otherwise scheduled. 
4. 
Electrically Operated Devices: Single manufacturer source for electric latch 
retraction devices, electrically controlled trim, power transfers, power supplies, 
monitoring switches and controls. 
5. 
Mullions: Anchors must be free and clear of expansion joints, consult Architect. 
Anchors into expansion joints will not be accepted. 
6. 
Accepted substitutions:  Precision Apex 2000 Series, Sargent 80 Series

PREPARATION 
 
A. 
Ensure that walls and frames are square and plumb before hardware installation.  Make

3.

to remain, match locations of existing hardware. 
 
C. 
Overhead stops: before installing, determine proposed locations of furniture items, 
fixtures, and other items to be protected by the overhead stop's action.  Install overhead 
stops prior to closers when both are used. 
 
3.3   
 
 
INSTALLATION 
 
A. 
Install hardware per manufacturer’s instructions and recommendations.  Do not install 
surface-mounted items until finishes have been completed on substrate or surface.  Set 
units level, plumb and true to line and location.  Adjust and reinforce attachment 
substrate or surface for proper installation and operation. Remove and reinstall or replace 
work deemed defective by NVARNG Lock Shop or Architect. 
1. 
Gaskets: install jamb-applied gaskets before closers, overhead stops, rim strikes, 
etc.; fasten hardware over and through these seals.  Install sweeps across 
bottoms of doors before astragals, cope sweeps around bottom pivots, trim 
astragals to tops of sweeps. 
2. 
When hardware is to be attached to existing metal surface and insufficient 
reinforcement exists, use RivNuts, NutSerts or similar anchoring device for 
screws as approved 
3. 
Use manufacturers' fasteners furnished with hardware items or submit Request 
for Substitution. 
4. 
Replace fasteners damaged by power-driven tools. 
5. 
Form fit/cut one-piece thresholds to match existing & new frame conditions. “H” 
shape on standard frames. 
 
B. 
Locate floor stops no more than 4 inches from walls and not within paths of travel.  See 
paragraph 2.2 regarding hinge widths, door should be well clear of point of wall reveal.  
Point of contact of floor stop to hinge edge of door to be not less than half the width of the 
door but not installed to offer a tripping hazard. Where situation is questionable or 
difficult, contact Architect or Architectural Hardware Consultant for direction. 
 
C. 
Exterior Floor Stops: Core concrete for exterior door floor stops. Set anchors or stops in 
epoxy.
